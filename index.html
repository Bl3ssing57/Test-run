About the Museum

A Vision Born
of Leadership


"Heritage is a form of knowledge — it must be documented, protected, and passed on."

Bathoen Gaseitsiwe, Kgosi Bathoen II of the Bangwaketse, was a rare figure: a paramount chief whose intellectual curiosity matched his political authority. Educated during the colonial era, he engaged deeply with natural history, taxonomy, and the documentation of wildlife — collecting and preserving specimens that spoke to the fauna of his land.

Over time his vision expanded. Cultural artefacts, oral traditions, and photographs of the Bangwaketse people joined his natural history holdings, unified by the belief that environment, history, and culture are inseparable. In the 1960s that vision became an institution — housed in a repurposed London Missionary Society building in Kanye, one of the earliest formal heritage spaces in Botswana.

What We Hold

Five Pillars
of Heritage

The museum's holdings reflect Kgosi Bathoen II's integrated worldview — that a people's story cannot be told without its land, its objects, its voices, and its images. Each collection is both archive and living resource for researchers, students, and communities.

🦁

Natural History

Mounted specimens, taxonomic records, and fauna of the Kalahari and surrounding region, reflecting the chief's early scientific practice.

🏺

Archaeology

Materials excavated from settlements across the region, tracing millennia of human presence in southern Botswana.

🧶

Ethnography

Cultural objects, tools, textiles, and everyday artefacts that document the lived practices of the Bangwaketse and neighbouring communities.

📷

Photography & Oral History

Photographs and recorded oral traditions preserving voices, faces, and stories that written archives could not capture alone.

A Living Legacy

From Personal Collection
to National Institution

The museum's story is one of vision, dormancy, and revival. Three moments define its arc — and each reflects the enduring relevance of Kgosi Bathoen II's original conviction.

1960s

The Founding

Bathoen Gaseitsiwe establishes the museum in a repurposed London Missionary Society building in Kanye — one of Botswana's first formal heritage institutions, born from a lifetime of collecting.

After Independence

A Period of Quiet

Following its initial flourishing, the museum enters a period of decline. The collections survive, awaiting those who would return them to public life.

1998

Revival

Community practitioner Abraham Kesupile leads the effort to restore the museum. The institution reopens as a public space for memory, learning, and cultural engagement — the original vision renewed.
